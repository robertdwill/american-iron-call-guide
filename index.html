1Frame min 0–3

"[Name], this is Robert. Can you hear me alright?"

"Are you married, or building this with a partner?"

  • No "Got it." Pitch them alone.
  • Yes, and they're on the call "Perfect." Use both names all call.
  • Yes, and they're NOT on the call no call. "We don't run this call without your [spouse/partner] on it. It's a decision that affects you both, and it saves you explaining it secondhand. Would [day] at [time] or [day] at [time] work for both of you?" Book it and end the call.

"Here's how this goes. I get your vision. Then we build your starting budget together on my screen, line by line. Then I show you how design gets you to a real builder bid. By the end you'll know your approximate budget and whether starting design makes sense right now. Sound fair?"

"One thing up front. We're a barndo design company, not the builder. We do the plans, and we connect you with vetted builders, barndo lenders and land agents, so you control your vision and your bids. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you straight and that's fine. Nobody can give a real price on a custom home before the design is finished, so everything today is a budget range. The hard number comes from builder bids on your finished plans."

Video check (the current pre-call video, "American Iron PreCall," about 5 minutes, at https://go.amiron.pro/video1): "Did you get a chance to watch the video I sent?"

If yes → "What stood out?" Then move on.

If no → they have to watch it. "No problem. It's about five minutes, so let's watch it together right now. It'll save us both time, and everything after it will make more sense." Then: "Any questions or thoughts on that?" (Those five minutes come out of the slot. Skip two Discovery questions, "How would living in this be different" and the cost-of-standing-still question, and keep Qualify to one line each. Do not shorten the Number or the Commit.)

2Qualify min 3–7

one question each, move fast

Existing designs. "By booking, I'm guessing you don't have completed designs yet, correct?"

  • Correct "That's the normal starting point."
  • They have some "Are those complete architectural plans? CAD files, plumbing, electrical, everything a builder could bid off of?"
    • Complete DQ. "Then you're past what we do. Take those straight to a builder. I'm not going to charge you for something you already own."
    • Incomplete say it flat, no "technically." "Concept drawings or a Pinterest folder won't get a real bid or a loan approval. We take what you have and make it buildable."

Land. "Do you own it, is it under contract, or are you still looking?"

  • Owns "Perfect. Has anyone confirmed the county or HOA allows this type of home? We'll price the site work in a minute."
  • Under contract "Design lets you check fit, slope, utilities and septic, with the right site professionals, before your earnest money goes non-refundable."
  • Looking "You don't need land to start design. Your design tells you what land to buy: footprint, slope, setbacks, septic. Your plans don't expire. And design takes eight to twelve weeks, so waiting for land only adds three months. We run them together and connect you with a land agent."

Then, every time: "So it's never a surprise later, the free-redesign guarantee starts once you own the land and you're pre-approved. Design itself doesn't wait on either."

Funding. "How are you funding this: cash, equity, a construction loan, or a mix?"

If a loan is involved → "Are you pre-approved yet?"

  • Pre-approved "Perfect, that clears the biggest hurdle."
  • Not yet "Traditional lending on these wants a 640 score, 5% down, 1–3% closing, and about $10k set aside for interest during the build. Are you about in that range?" (same guarantee flag as land)
  • No path DQ unless they choose to start anyway. Give the price and let them decide. "We can still get designs started at $2 a square foot, and designs give you clarity on what you want and what needs approval. Or we pause here. What puts you in the best position?"

Builder. "Do you already have a builder in mind, or would you want help finding one once the plans are ready?" (If they name one, the local-builder line in section 8 is ready.)

Timeline. "Designs take eight to twelve weeks, and the whole project runs 6–15 months. When do you want keys?"

6–15 months fine. Further out is not a DQ: plans don't expire. Count backward from their date live so they see when design has to start, then go on. Then end Dated, with a follow-up booked for a date tied to when design has to start. If they won't book one, use the real-no line in section 8.

3Discovery min 7–15

ask, then stop talking

Vision. "I've got your form. [STATE], about [X] square feet. Do I have that right?"

"Paint me the picture. What are you trying to get built?"

"What do you have to have, or you'll be disappointed?"

"What made now the time?"

"How would living in this be different from where you are now?"

The wrong path. Ask first. "You're going to run into people selling a cheaper way to start. What have you heard about how it goes when the plans aren't finished before the build starts?"

Let them tell it. Then add ONE line, the one closest to their worry (not all four):

  • Change orders: thirty to a hundred thousand in change orders and contingency overruns.
  • Permits: two to six months lost when the county kicks back incomplete plans.
  • Financing: lenders won't fund incomplete documentation.
  • Foundation: nobody engineered for that lot's slope or soil.

"Which of those worries you most for your project?"

One cost-of-standing-still question, not three: "If nothing changes and you're in the same spot a year from now, what does that mean for the family?"

4The Number min 15–22

screen share, estimator live

Estimator: https://americanironplans.pages.dev/estimator

"Let's build an approximate starting budget. I'll use your numbers and you'll see every line."

Living [sq ft] → garage/shop [sq ft] → porches [sq ft] → site work.

  • Owns land "Your land's handled. What site work do you expect?"
  • Looking price land live from comps on the shared screen.

"All in, you're at about $[X]. That's a starting budget, not a quote. How does that feel against what you had in mind?"

  • Fits "Then the math works and this is a real project."
  • Higher don't justify, ask. "Where were you? So we're $[X] apart. Three ways to close it: square footage, finish level, or phase the garage and porches later. Which is easiest to give on?" Rebuild live. Land inside their number and say it: "There. That's under where you said you needed to be."
  • Never end on an unresolved gap. Try at least one live scope cut first.

5Sequence + Belief Check min 22–26

"Here's the order, and it only goes one direction. A lender can't fund a guess, and a builder can't price a guess. Design comes first because it's the only thing that produces a real number. After that, a builder bids it for real and a bank funds it for real."

Belief check (skip if they already said it in their own words): "Would it feel fair to say that holding a 100% bank-ready, engineered plan set before you sign a construction contract or pay for dirt would make this a lot easier, and a lot safer for your family?"

"What you get is a complete builder-ready design: full architectural, engineering, a permit-ready submittal, and documentation a lender can underwrite. Take it to our vetted builders or any builder you want, and have them bid the same drawings. And we stay with you as your advocate, dirt to keys."

"The plans are yours. The full CAD set, no expiration. Use any builder, sit on it three years, it's yours."

6Present min 26–31

the portfolio site

Pull, in this order, and tie each to something they said:

1. Portfolio https://americanironplans.pages.dev (home page) — filter to their square-foot band, beds and shop. Two or three homes, not ten. Open the render gallery of the closest one.

2. The one nearest their must-have: "You said [RV bay]. Look at how this one handled it."

3. Documents https://americanironplans.pages.dev/construction-documents — real delivered plan sets. "This is the level a builder bids from."

4. Engineering https://americanironplans.pages.dev/structural-engineering — only if they raised builders cutting corners or materials. "An engineered set is a binding spec. Any substitution has to be reviewed and approved."

Say: "This is someone else's layout and yours won't look like it. Yours is custom. This is the level of detail you're getting." Sizes on the portfolio cards are estimates, so don't quote them as built sizes.

Then a statement: "That's exactly why families come to us instead of piecing it together themselves."

Temperature. "One is 'not for me,' ten is 'this is exactly what I want.' Where are you?"

Ten → commit. Below ten → "What's missing for you?" Ask once, answer that one thing, then commit, date it, or DQ. Don't loop for a ten.

7Commit min 31–45

the close gets the most time

Bridge. "You told me [their why, verbatim]. We built a starting budget at $[TOTAL]. You've seen the level of design you'd get. The only thing between you and a real builder bid is the design. Want me to explain how that works?"

Ask. "The design package is $2 per square foot. To get started it's $4,000 down, our minimum design fee regardless of square footage. When plans are final and we know exact scope, we invoice the difference at $2 a square foot, so you're not paying for footage you haven't decided on."

"We also do free unlimited redesigns if builders come back higher than your top budget because of finishes you chose. As many times as it takes. Two conditions, said plainly: you're pre-approved, and you own your land. Until both are true there's no real budget or site to design against."

"Does this put you in the best position to get the barndo you want at your budget?" → wait → "Why do you feel that way?" → "How do you want to proceed?"

Transact. "Sending the link to [email] now. Tell me when it lands." Stay on until it reads paid. Fill the gap with onboarding questions. Doubt surfaces → "What's coming up for you?" and handle that one thing.

Cleared → "That's in. Onboarding email today with a short form on your must-haves, and I'm booking you with your account manager now to pair you with your designer. Design runs eight to twelve weeks."

Can't clear on the call → book a date and time to finish it.

Downsell (logistics or scope uncertainty, not a value objection): "What we can do is at least get the preliminaries started with design and work on finals from there once you work through more details. To get started there it's just $2K down. Would that work?"

8When They Stall

isolate, answer that one thing, never argue
  • "I need to think about it." "Not a problem. Is it the layout you need to feel out, or the timing of the deposit?" Layout "What part isn't sitting right?" then back to 6. Deposit → downsell. Neither → two specific times, never open-ended.
  • "It's too expensive." "I hear you. Is it the $4,000 to start, or the total build number?" Total "That's the starting budget we built together. Finished plans are what make real bids possible. If bids come in higher than you can live with, what would you want to do?" $4,000 + pre-approved → "The design fee can be written in as a line item and rolled into the construction loan, credited back once funded, like earnest money." $4,000, no loan → downsell.
  • "I need to run it by someone else." Your partner is already on the call by rule, so this is a third person (a parent, a business partner, a lender). "Besides you [and partner], who else has a say in this?" Then: "If they were sitting here and felt completely confident, would you be ready to move forward?" Yes book a joint time, two options. No → "What would they need to see?" then a dated follow-up. Never close a joint decision without everyone who decides.
  • "Why go through you? I know local builders." (came up on two recent calls) "Totally fair. Use them. Take the finished plans to any builder you want, ours or yours, and have them bid the same drawings. You're not locked to us."
  • "[Competitor] quoted us less." Ask what their number includes (materials, foundation, labor, permits) before answering. Never wave it off. (observed once; this miss lost a deal)
  • "My [family member] can do it." Don't compete. Give them questions to ask: "Do they specialize in barndos? Will their plans carry a lender-required stamp?" (observed once)
  • Real no. "No problem at all. I wish you the best. If anything changes or you'd like a second opinion down the road, I'm a message away." No last offer, no "are you sure."

9Three Endings

there is no fourth

Paid — deposit or prelim cleared on the call.

Dated — specific date and time on the calendar. "Assuming you feel the same way [day], how would you want to handle the design investment when we talk?"

Disqualified — you told them straight it's not a fit, and why.
