The wrong path
Ask first.
You're going to run into people selling a cheaper way to start. What have you heard about how it goes when the plans aren't finished before the build starts?
Let them tell it. Then add ONE line, the one closest to their worry (not all four):
Change orders: thirty to a hundred thousand in change orders and contingency overruns.
Permits: two to six months lost when the county kicks back incomplete plans.
Financing: lenders won't fund incomplete documentation.
Foundation: nobody engineered for that lot's slope or soil.
Which of those worries you most for your project?
One cost-of-standing-still question, not three:
If nothing changes and you're in the same spot a year from now, what does that mean for the family?

Step 5 · minutes 22–26

Sequence + Belief Check

Here's the order, and it only goes one direction. A lender can't fund a guess, and a builder can't price a guess. Design comes first because it's the only thing that produces a real number. After that, a builder bids it for real and a bank funds it for real.
Belief check
(skip if they already said it in their own words):
Would it feel fair to say that holding a 100% bank-ready, engineered plan set before you sign a construction contract or pay for dirt would make this a lot easier, and a lot safer for your family?
What you get is a complete builder-ready design: full architectural, engineering, a permit-ready submittal, and documentation a lender can underwrite. Take it to our vetted builders or any builder you want, and have them bid the same drawings. And we stay with you as your advocate, dirt to keys.
The plans are yours. The full CAD set, no expiration. Use any builder, sit on it three years, it's yours.
