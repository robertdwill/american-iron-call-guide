THE FRAME
First off, this isn't a sales presentation. It's a high-level conversation about where you're at in your land and barndo journey and where you want to go. If it all lines up, I'll tell you straight, and we'll talk about what's next.
Here's how the next 45 minutes go. I'll get your build vision, what you're actually trying to end up with. Then we'll build your entry budget together, live, on my screen, so you see what this would cost as a starting point before choosing finishings and other details. Then I'll show you what this looks like to design and get built, and how you get from here to keys in hand.
By the end you'll know two things: what your approximate starting budget would look like, and whether starting design makes sense for you right now. Sound fair?
One thing up front so nothing's a surprise. We're a barndo design company, not always the builder. We specialize in barndos and do plans. We have a network of vetted builders, lending partners for barndos, and connections with land agents. We put families in control of the bidding process and connected with reputable builders, so you control your vision and cost.
A lot of people show up holding a piece of paper and say, we want a barndo that looks something like this. A builder can't quote off that. Hand it to three builders and you get three wildly different numbers.
Nobody, not us, not any builder, can give you a real price on a custom home without completed designs first. Everything today is a budget range. The hard number comes from builder bids off your finished design. That's not us being cagey, it's just the sequence. We'll get into it. So we design first, and every builder bids the same thing, apples to apples. Do you agree?

Looking / no land
Completely fine, and I want to be clear about something a lot of people get backwards: you do not need land to start design.
Three reasons.
One: your design tells you what land to buy. Footprint, slope tolerance, setbacks, septic. People buy a lot first, then find out their layout doesn't fit, and they eat that.
Two: your plans don't expire. They're yours, CAD files and all, whether you build next spring or in three years.
Three: design takes eight to twelve weeks. Wait for land and you've added three months for no reason. They run in parallel, and we'll connect you with a land agent.
One thing to know now so it's never a surprise later: our free-redesign guarantee starts once you own the land and you're pre-approved. Design itself doesn't wait on either one.

Whatever they say is what you close on. Then add only ONE thing they left out, the one closest to their worry (two at most), one line each:
Change orders

Permits
Two to six months of delays, because plans that aren't submittal-ready get kicked back by the county.
Financing
Lenders won't fund incomplete documentation. People get to closing and the loan dies.
Foundation
Nobody engineered for the slope or soil on that specific lot.
Which of those worries you most for your project and budget?

Step 4 · minutes 24–28

Education

Ask permission, then the sequence

Based on what you've told me, would it be okay if I walked you through how we'd take this from paper to a real build?
Block A — The sequence lock
Here's the sequence, and it only goes one direction. A lender can't fund a guess, and a builder can't price a guess. Design comes first because it's the only thing that produces a real number. It's not because we sell design. Once you have complete plans, a builder bids it for real and a bank funds it for real. Before that, everything anybody tells you is a guess dressed up as a quote.
Does that line up with what you've researched?
Block B — Design+, plainly, and show example plan photos again
What you get is a complete builder-ready design: full architectural, engineering done if needed, permit-ready submittal the county can approve, and financing-ready documentation so your loan closes.
Then we take that design to our builder network, vetted builders who are insured and licensed. You can bring these designs to any builder you want as well and have them bid against each other on your actual plans. Not a lowball to win your business and change-order you to death. A real bid off real drawings.
And we stay with you as your advocate through the build. Dirt to keys.
Belief check (skip if they already said it in their own words)
First, let them say it
What would you want in your hands before you sit down with any builder?
Then confirm it
Would it be fair to say that having a 100% bank-ready, engineered plan set in your hands before you sign a construction contract or pay for dirt would make this a lot easier, and a lot safer for your family?
Then
Why do you feel that way?
Block C — Risk reversal (unprompted, before they ask)
Be clear about what you're getting: the plans are yours. CAD files, the whole set. Builder outside our network, use them. Sit on it three years, use them, they don't expire. You're not renting a design from us.

Step 6 · minutes 33–45

Commit

Bridge, ask, transact

6a · The bridge (their words → the sequence → the ask, no gap between them)
So here's where we are. You told me [THEIR WHY]. We built a starting budget that works at [ALL-IN]. You've seen the level of design you'd be getting. The only thing between you and a real builder bid is the design itself. Want me to explain how that works?
6b · Permission
Their answer: YES
6c · The ask
So this design package is just $2 per square foot of total square footage under roof: living space, garage or shop, and porches. To get design started it's $4,000 down, our minimum design fee regardless of square footage. Once your plans are finalized and we know exact scope, we invoice the difference at $2 a square foot. So you're not paying for square footage you haven't decided on yet.
Plus, we do free unlimited redesigns if builders come back higher than your top budget because of finishes you chose, or you got attached to a countertop, haha. As many times as it takes to bring you back into budget. Not one revision, however many it takes.
Two conditions on that, and I want to say them plainly so there's no confusion later: you need to be pre-approved, and you need to own your land. Those two things have to be true for the guarantee to apply, because until you're approved and you've got a lot, there's no real budget and no real site to design against, so there's nothing for me to redesign you back into.
Do you feel like this process puts you guys in the best position possible to get the barndo you want to your budget?
